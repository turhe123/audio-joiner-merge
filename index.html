    How it works

    1. Add your clips. Drop audio files on the page or pick them. Each one is read on your device to show its length; nothing is uploaded.
    2. Set the order. Drag the rows or use the arrow buttons, then choose a gap or crossfade, MP3 or WAV, and a file name.
    3. Join and save. Press Join. The clips are decoded, resampled to the first file's rate, stitched and encoded in your browser. Listen, then download.

    Questions, answered

    Are my audio files uploaded anywhere?

    No. Audio Joiner and Merger runs entirely in your browser. Your files are decoded and joined in memory on your device and never sent to a server. You can disconnect from the internet and it keeps working.

    Which formats can I join?

    Anything your browser can decode: MP3, WAV, OGG, Opus, and usually M4A, AAC and FLAC. Some M4A or FLAC files will not open in every browser; those rows are marked and skipped. Files may have different sample rates and channel counts: everything is converted to the first file's rate, and mono clips play on both channels.

    How do the gap and crossfade work?

    A gap inserts silence between clips. A crossfade overlaps the end of one clip with the start of the next and fades between them, so a 2 second crossfade makes the result about 2 seconds shorter per join. The volume of each clip is kept as it is: there is no loudness normalization, so very quiet and very loud clips stay that way.

    Is there a size limit?

    Only your device's memory. Joined audio is held uncompressed while it is built, so about 2 hours in total is a sensible ceiling; phones may manage less. There are no accounts, quotas or watermarks.

    Is it really free? Does it work offline?

    Yes and yes. It is open source under the MIT license, with no account, no limits and no ads. After your first visit a service worker caches the page so it loads without a connection, and you can install it from your browser's menu. If it saves you a paid app, you can say thanks on Ko-fi.
